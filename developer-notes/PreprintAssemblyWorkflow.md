# 🧩 Developer Note — Preprint Assembly Workflow

Author: Borealis Serenity Hedling

Purpose: Document the workflow for assembling the FSFE preprint from altitude modules, guard specifications, consistency modules, and Lean appendices.

---

1. Overview

The FSFE preprint is not a monolithic document — it is a structured assembly of altitude modules (A6–A8), guard specifications, consistency conditions, and Lean theorem signatures.

This Developer Note documents:

- how the preprint is constructed  
- how altitude modules are integrated  
- how guard specifications are inserted  
- how consistency modules are woven in  
- how Lean appendices are attached  
- how citations and definitions are tightened  
- how the final preprint is produced  

This workflow ensures the preprint remains altitude‑safe, academically defensible, and formally verifiable.

---

2. Preprint Structure

The FSFE preprint follows a strict altitude‑aligned structure:

`
1. Title + Abstract
2. Altitude Overview
3. Altitude A6 — Scalar-Field Geometry
4. Altitude A7 — Invariant Layer
5. Altitude A8 — Holonomy-Runtime Mathematics
6. Cross-Altitude Consistency
7. Guard Specifications
8. Lean Appendix
9. Bibliography
`

Each section corresponds to a specific altitude or cross‑altitude module.

---

3. Assembly Workflow

Step 1 — Insert Preprint Skeleton
Start with the altitude‑clean skeleton:

- title  
- abstract  
- table of contents  
- section headers  

This ensures the document is structurally correct before content is added.

---

Step 2 — Insert Altitude Modules (A6 → A7 → A8)

Each altitude module is inserted into its corresponding section:

- A6: scalar fields, evolution operators, collapse surfaces, spectral ecology, projection seeds  
- A7: operator invariants, collapse invariants, fusion invariants, manifold‑coupling invariants  
- A8: holonomy operators, curvature evolution, spectral integration, runtime fusion, constraints  

Altitude modules must be inserted in order, never interleaved.

---

Step 3 — Insert Tightened Definitions

Definitions must be:

- altitude‑local  
- mathematically defensible  
- citation‑supported  
- notation‑consistent  

Tightened definitions replace informal ones.

---

Step 4 — Insert Citation Hooks

Citations are inserted at:

- geometric definitions (A6)  
- invariant definitions (A7)  
- holonomy definitions (A8)  

Citations must reference authoritative mathematical sources.

---

Step 5 — Insert Cross‑Altitude Links

Each altitude section ends with a “Altitude Link” paragraph explaining:

- how the altitude depends on the one below  
- how it constrains the one above  

This prevents altitude drift.

---

Step 6 — Insert Guard Specifications

Guard specifications are inserted as their own section:

- operator grounding  
- invariant grounding  
- scalar‑field grounding  
- holonomy grounding  

Guards enforce altitude boundaries.

---

Step 7 — Insert Consistency Modules

Consistency modules bind altitudes:

- A6 → A7  
- A7 → A8  
- A6 → A8  

These modules must reference theorem signatures, not definitions.

---

Step 8 — Insert Lean Appendix

The Lean appendix includes:

- altitude theorem signatures  
- consistency theorem signatures  
- Lean module structure  
- proof‑prep references  

This ensures the preprint and Lean formalization remain aligned.

---

Step 9 — Insert Bibliography

Bibliography includes:

- geometry references  
- invariant theory references  
- holonomy references  
- spectral/evolution references  

This ensures academic defensibility.

---

4. Altitude Safety Rules for Preprint Assembly

Rule 1 — No Cross‑Altitude Definitions
Definitions must remain inside their altitude.

Rule 2 — Consistency Modules Bind, Not Merge
Consistency modules reference theorem signatures only.

Rule 3 — Guards Enforce Boundaries
Guards must appear after consistency modules.

Rule 4 — Lean Appendix Must Be Last
Lean content must not appear inside altitude sections.

Rule 5 — Citations Must Be Altitude‑Local
Geometry cites geometry; invariants cite invariant theory; holonomy cites holonomy.
