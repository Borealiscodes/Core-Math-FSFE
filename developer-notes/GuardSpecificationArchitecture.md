# 🛡️ Developer Note — Guard Specification Architecture

Author: Borealis Serenity Hedling  
Date: 29 October 2026  
Module: FSFE Guard System  
Purpose: Document the architecture, purpose, and enforcement role of guard specifications across altitudes A6–A8.

---

1. Overview

Guard specifications are the enforcement layer of the FSFE altitude system.  
They ensure that:

- operators behave correctly within their altitude  
- invariants remain preserved across transformations  
- holonomy-runtime operators respect geometric and invariant constraints  
- altitude boundaries remain intact  
- consistency modules have enforceable conditions  

Guards are not mathematical objects themselves — they are structural constraints that bind altitude modules together without collapsing them.

---

2. Purpose of Guards

Guards exist to:

- prevent altitude drift  
- enforce invariant preservation  
- ensure geometric regularity is respected  
- constrain holonomy-runtime behavior  
- provide altitude‑safe bindings between modules  
- support formal verification by making constraints explicit  

They are the “runtime safety rails” of the FSFE architecture.

---

3. Guard Categories

FSFE defines four guard categories, each corresponding to a specific altitude boundary.

3.1 Operator Grounding Guards (A7 ↔ A8)

These guards ensure:

- reversible operators (A7) remain invariant‑safe under holonomy (A8)  
- fusion operators (A7) remain reversible under runtime fusion (A8)  
- operator invariants are preserved during parallel transport  

They enforce the rule:

`
Holonomy must respect A7 invariants.
`

3.2 Invariant Grounding Guards (A6 ↔ A7)

These guards ensure:

- collapse-surface invariants remain stable  
- fusion invariants remain altitude‑consistent  
- manifold-coupling invariants remain metric‑consistent  

They enforce the rule:

`
A7 invariants must be compatible with A6 geometry.
`

3.3 Scalar-Field Grounding Guards (A6)

These guards ensure:

- evolution operators preserve smoothness  
- collapse surfaces remain regular  
- spectral coefficients remain normalized  
- projection seeds preserve geometry  

They enforce the rule:

`
A6 geometry must remain altitude‑safe.
`

3.4 Holonomy Grounding Guards (A7 ↔ A8)

These guards ensure:

- holonomy operators preserve invariants  
- curvature evolution respects geometric constraints  
- spectral integration remains normalized  
- runtime fusion remains reversible  

They enforce the rule:

`
Holonomy-runtime operators must remain invariant‑safe and geometry‑compatible.
`

4. Guard Specification Structure

Each guard specification has the following structure:

1. Altitude Context  
   Which altitude(s) the guard applies to.

2. Bound Objects  
   The mathematical objects being constrained.

3. Constraint Statement  
   The altitude‑safe condition that must hold.

4. Lean Binding  
   The theorem signature or lemma spine the guard references.

5. Consistency Role  
   How the guard contributes to cross‑altitude consistency.

This structure ensures guards remain:

- explicit  
- enforceable  
- altitude‑local  
- formally verifiable  

---

5. Guard Enforcement Model

The correct conceptual model is:

`
A6 Geometry
    ↑ enforced by scalar-field guards
A7 Invariants
    ↑ enforced by invariant grounding guards
A8 Holonomy-Runtime
    ↑ enforced by operator + holonomy grounding guards
Consistency
    ↑ enforced by cross-altitude guard bindings
`

Guards enforce altitude separation without merging altitudes.

---

6. Guard Interaction with Lean

Guards interact with Lean in three ways:

1. They bind theorem signatures across altitudes.
Lean uses guard specifications to determine which constraints must be proved.

2. They define the structure of consistency modules.
Consistency modules reference guard constraints, not altitude definitions.

3. They prevent circular imports.
Guards ensure Lean modules remain altitude‑local.
